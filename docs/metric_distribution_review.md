## Scope & method

Reviews the **48 distinct source metrics** that feed the dashboard's
**5 thematic bundles** (Heat Risk, Heat Stress, Drought Risk,
Extreme Rainfall | Flash Flood Risk, Cold Risk) and **8 sector bundles**
(Agricultural, Health, Industrial, Investment/Financial, Infrastructure,
Asset-Thermal, Asset-Hydropower, Life & Livelihood Loss).

- **Slice audited:** `historical / 1990-2010 / ensemble-mean`, **district** level.
- **Coverage:** all 35–36 states/UTs, **781 districts** pooled nationwide.
- **Column contract:** `{value_col}__{scenario}__{period}__{stat}`
  (e.g. `txx_annual_max_C__historical__1990-2010__mean`).
- **Stats artifact (reproducible):** `metric_dist_audit.csv` — per-metric
  `n_units, nan_pct, min/p05/median/mean/p95/max/std`, plus top-3 / bottom-3
  extreme districts. Generated by the standalone script
  `metric_dist_audit.py` (staged in scratch during the audit; not yet
  committed to `tools/`).
- **Environment:** analysis must run under the Windows `irt` conda env
  (`.../envs/irt/python.exe`, geopandas 1.1.3). WSL `python3` has no pandas.
  Windows paths: `D:\projects\irt_data\processed\{slug}\{state}\master_metrics_by_district.parquet`.

## Headline verdict

**Core compute is sound.** Every metric's geography is physically correct —
Meghalaya (Cherrapunji) tops all rainfall metrics; Bihar/Gangetic plains top
wet-bulb; coastal Tamil Nadu tops tropical nights; Gujarat/Vidarbha top dry
heat; the Himalaya bottoms everything. Wet-bulb maxes cap at ~31 °C (no
impossible >35 °C). No unit errors or sign flips. **43 of 48 metrics: sensible,
no action.** Five need a decision (below) — none is a silent numeric bug; all
are methodology/coverage choices.

## FLAG A — Percentile-exceedance indices are inert in the historical slice

**Status:** RESOLVED via **Option 1 (annotate)** — 2026-07-13.
Registry `description` for all four metrics now carries a plain-language note
that they are near-flat (~10 %) by construction in the historical baseline and
carry real signal only under future warming scenarios; this renders in the
UI metric-picker "About this metric" tooltip (`app/ribbon.py`). No scoring or
methodology change. See CHG-0226/CHG-0227.

**Metrics:** `tx90p_hot_days_pct`, `tn90p_warm_nights_pct`,
`tx10p_cool_days_pct`, `tn10p_cool_nights_pct`.

**Evidence:** all four sit at **9.3–10.4 % nationwide**, total spread ≤0.25 pp.
- `tx90p`: 10.23 (Punjab) – 10.41 (Kerala)
- `tn90p`: 10.18 – 10.40
- `tx10p`: 9.32 – 9.45
- `tn10p`: 9.23 – 9.45

**Future-slice confirmation (audit re-run 2026-07-13, all 781 districts, 0 % NaN):**
the flatness is historical-only; the future signal is large, monotonic in
scenario+time, and geographically physical (low-variability tropical islands
cross their own threshold on ~99 % of days; high-seasonal-spread interior MP
dilutes toward 50 %).

| metric | historical mean (range) | ssp585 2060–2080 mean (range) |
|---|---|---|
| `tx90p_hot_days_pct` | 10.32 % (0.18 pp) | **59.9 %** (48.8 pp) |
| `tn90p_warm_nights_pct` | 10.29 % (0.22 pp) | **70.5 %** (41.7 pp) |
| `tx10p_cool_days_pct` | 9.38 % (0.13 pp) | **1.8 %** (3.3 pp) |
| `tn10p_cool_nights_pct` | 9.34 % (0.22 pp) | **4.6 %** (5.8 pp) |

Reproduced by `flag_a_future_audit.py` (staged in scratch during the audit;
writes `flag_a_future_audit.csv`).

**Scoring-path note (why the historical flatness is not merely inert):**
`analysis/bundle_scores.py::normalize_metric_series` is min–max across the
frame — it stretches `[min, max] → [0, 100]` regardless of whether the spread
is signal or noise. On the historical slice this stretches ~0.2 pp of noise to
the full 0–100 range at ~7–10 % bundle weight. Option 1 documents this rather
than changing it; the default landing view is a **future** slice
(`ssp585 / 2040-2060`, `app/landing_runtime.py:61`) where the metrics are
legitimate, so the residual noise only bites when a user explicitly selects the
historical slice. A signal-blind-normalization guard was considered and
deferred (would be a methodology change).

**Root cause (not a bug):** these count exceedances of each district's **own
baseline percentile threshold** (local/relative, computed per calendar day).
Evaluated over ≈the baseline window, every district exceeds its own 90th/10th
percentile ~10 %/~10 % of the time *by definition*. Hotness/coldness is
normalized away, so the between-district differences are residual noise from
the eval window (1990-2010) not being byte-identical to the threshold baseline
(1981-2010 for the 90p indices, 1990-2010 for the 10p indices) plus
calendar/rounding effects — **not** a real spatial signal.

**Impact:** in the **historical** composite/ranking these add ~zero spatial
discrimination (ranking by them ranks noise). Their genuine signal appears only
in **future** scenarios (ssp245/ssp585), where exceedance climbs above 10 %.

**Decision options:**
1. Keep, understanding they are *future-signal* metrics (annotate in UI/docs).
2. Drop them from the **historical** ranking/composite, keep for future slices.
3. Re-anchor eval baseline so historical ≡ baseline (makes them exactly ~10 %,
   confirming they carry no historical signal — mostly a confirmation step).

**Next step to confirm:** re-run the audit on ssp245/ssp585 future periods and
verify the spread widens and the pattern becomes geographically meaningful.

## FLAG B — SPI3 missing in 34 arid NW districts

**Metrics:** `spi3_count_events_lt_minus1`, `spi3_max_spell_lt_minus1`,
`spi3_count_months_lt_minus1` (all show **4.4 % NaN**). SPI6/SPI12 have **0 % NaN**.

**Evidence:** the 34 NaN districts localize entirely to arid NW India —
**Rajasthan (18), Gujarat (10), Madhya Pradesh (5), Maharashtra (1).**
(Bottom valid SPI3 districts are also Gujarat: Tapi 2.50, Surat 2.51.)

**Root cause (scientifically legitimate limitation):** classic
SPI-in-arid-zones degeneracy. In the Thar/arid belt many 3-month precip windows
sum to ≈0, so the gamma fit degenerates / the coverage gate
(`min_months_per_year=9`, `min_baseline_years_per_calendar_month_fraction=0.83`,
`min_polygon_cell_weight_fraction=0.50`) trips → NaN. Longer scales (6/12-month)
accumulate enough non-zero precip to survive, hence 0 NaN.

**Impact:** Drought Risk and Agricultural Risk lose their SPI3 component
**exactly in the driest districts** — where drought risk matters most. Missing
components can then trip the sector bundles' `available_rule_weight_fraction`
coverage gate (0.70), dropping or reweighting those units.

**Decision options:**
1. Accept + annotate as a known SPI limitation in hyper-arid zones.
2. Relax / redesign the SPI3 coverage gate for arid seasonality.
3. Substitute an arid-robust index (empirical percentile, SPEI, or
   zero-inflated SPI) where SPI3 degenerates.

**Next step to confirm:** trace the exact step in
`compute/drought_risk_gridfirst.py` (gamma fit vs coverage gate) that emits NaN
for e.g. Jaisalmer; quantify the downstream coverage-gate cascade into the
Drought and Agricultural composites.

## FLAG C — JRC RP-100 flood index: NaN coverage gaps + floor/ceiling saturation

**Metric:** `jrc_flood_depth_index_rp100`
(column `jrc_flood_depth_index_rp100__snapshot__Current__mean`), feeds the
**Riverine Flood** thematic bundle. 1–5 severity index.

**Evidence:**
- **5.9 % NaN**, concentrated in **J&K (21), Arunachal Pradesh (15),
  Andaman & Nicobar (3), Nagaland (3), Ladakh (1), Lakshadweep (1),
  Manipur (1), Punjab (1)**.
- **17.6 % of districts pinned at floor 1.0; 10.8 % pinned at ceiling 5.0**
  (p95 = max = 5.0). Assam floodplain districts all = 5.
- Bottom valid: WB Paschim Bardhaman, Uttarakhand Pithoragarh/Rudra Prayag = 1.0.

**Root cause:** the NaN states are where the **per-state JRC builder hasn't run
yet** (this is the `add_flood_depth` branch, mid-migration) or the JRC layer
lacks coverage — a build-scope/coverage issue, **not** a physics gap. The
floor/ceiling pileup is inherent to a coarse 1–5 discretization.

**Impact:** the index cannot discriminate among high-flood districts (all
Assam = 5) or among low-flood districts (many = 1). Riverine Flood ranking
is compressed at both tails, and missing states have no flood signal at all.

**Decision options:**
1. Finish JRC builds for the missing states, or explicitly mark them "no data".
2. Reconsider granularity — continuous depth (m) vs a 5-bucket index — to
   restore discrimination at the tails.

**Next step to confirm:** check which states have `jrc_flood_depth/{state}/`
masters vs the boundary roster; decide build backlog vs no-data marking.

## FLAG D — "Heatwave amplitude" semantics (definition/labelling)

**Metric:** `hwa_heatwave_amplitude` (`hwa_peak_temp_C`), Heat Risk bundle.

**Evidence:** median 36.4 °C, but **bottom districts report 11.8 °C
(Lahul & Spiti), 12.8 °C (Leh), 13.6 °C (Mangan)** as "heatwave amplitude".
Top: Jalgaon 40.3, Anand/Kheda 40.2 (correct).

**Root cause (internally consistent):** amplitude is defined as the **absolute
peak Tmax within the hottest spell**, where a spell is defined by a **relative**
(local DOY-90p) threshold. In cold high-altitude districts the local threshold
is low, so a "heatwave" can peak at ~12 °C. The metric conflates *how hot*
(absolute) with *how anomalous* (relative event definition).

**Impact:** for heat-risk **ranking** it behaves fine (like an absolute-temp
metric — cold districts correctly rank low). The issue is **semantic/labelling**:
calling a 12 °C day a "heatwave peak" is misleading in cold regions.

**Decision options:**
1. Keep metric, clarify the label/description (absolute peak within a
   relatively-defined spell).
2. Switch to a magnitude-of-exceedance definition (the commented-out
   `hwm_heatwave_magnitude` = mean exceedance above threshold) if an
   anomaly-based amplitude is intended.

## FLAG E — Split baselines inside the percentile family (static / registry)

**Evidence (from `config/metrics_registry.py`, not from data):**
- **1981–2010** baseline: `tx90p`, `tn90p`, `wsdi_warm_spell_days`,
  `hwfi_tmean_90p`, `hwfi_events_tmean_90p`, `hwa_heatwave_amplitude`,
  R95p/R99p/R95pTOT, SPI3/6/12.
- **1990–2010** baseline: `tx10p`, `tn10p`, `csdi_cold_spell_days`.

**Impact:** warm-tail and cool-tail percentile indices are **not strictly
comparable** — they reference different climate normals. Not a distribution
defect; surfaces when comparing cool vs warm percentile indices side by side.

**Decision:** the deferred universal-baseline (1990–2010) harmonization —
must be done deliberately, not silently, and re-tested (methodology change).
See the standing "universal baseline 1990–2010" code-gap note.
